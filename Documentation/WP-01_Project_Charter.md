# WP-01: Project Charter

## Inter IKEA Group Financial Performance Analysis

**Analysis Period:** FY23–FY25  
**Prepared by:** Neha R K  
**Role:** Financial Analyst — Independent Portfolio Case Study  
**Project Status:** In Progress  
**Prepared:** July 2026  

---

## 1. Project Overview

This independent portfolio project evaluates the financial performance of Inter IKEA Group over FY23, FY24 and FY25 using publicly available annual reports.

The project is designed to demonstrate practical skills relevant to accounting internships, junior accountant positions, financial analyst internships and junior financial analyst roles.

The analysis will combine financial statement analysis, ratio analysis, trend analysis, business interpretation and data visualization.

---

## 2. Company Analysed

**Company:** Inter IKEA Group  
**Parent Entity:** Inter IKEA Holding B.V.  
**Industry:** Home Furnishings, Franchise Operations, Product Development and Supply Chain  
**Reporting Currency:** Euro  
**Financial Year-End:** 31 August  

Inter IKEA Group operates through three core business areas:

1. Retail Concept
2. Range
3. Supply

The Group owns and develops the IKEA Concept, creates the IKEA product range and manages sourcing, manufacturing and wholesale supply activities for IKEA franchisees.

This project analyses Inter IKEA Group and should not be interpreted as an analysis of every independently operated IKEA retail franchise.

---

## 3. Business Problem

Management and finance stakeholders require a clear understanding of how the Group's financial performance and financial position changed between FY23 and FY25.

The analysis must explain:

- Whether operating income is growing or declining
- Whether profitability is improving
- How cost movements affected earnings
- Whether liquidity remains adequate
- Whether the capital structure is financially stable
- Whether operating activities are generating sufficient cash
- Which business and financial risks require attention
- Which factors explain significant year-over-year movements

---

## 4. Primary Analytical Question

How did Inter IKEA Group's financial performance change between FY23 and FY25, and what operational, economic and strategic factors explain those changes?

---

## 5. Supporting Business Questions

### Revenue and Growth

- How did total operating income change over the three-year period?
- Which pricing, volume and business events influenced operating income?
- Was growth consistent across the period?

### Profitability

- How did operating profit and net profit change?
- Which operating costs had the greatest effect on profitability?
- Did profit margins improve or weaken?
- How did financial income, financial expenses and taxation affect net profit?

### Financial Position

- How did total assets, liabilities and equity change?
- Did the Group maintain a strong equity position?
- Were major changes caused by normal operations or exceptional transactions?

### Liquidity and Working Capital

- Can the Group meet its short-term obligations?
- How did cash, receivables, inventories and current liabilities change?
- What effect did inventory movements have on working capital?

### Cash Flow

- Did operating activities generate positive cash flow?
- How was operating cash used?
- Were investments and dividends supported by internally generated cash?

### Efficiency

- Was the Group using its assets efficiently?
- Did inventory management improve or weaken?
- Did working-capital movements support or reduce cash generation?

### Risk

- What financial, operational and external risks could affect future performance?
- How significant are supply-chain, commodity-price, foreign-exchange, tariff and interest-rate risks?

---

## 6. Project Objectives

The objectives of this project are to:

- Extract and standardize three years of financial-statement data
- Evaluate year-over-year financial performance
- Calculate relevant financial ratios and key performance indicators
- Identify significant financial and operational trends
- Connect management commentary with reported financial results
- Build a clear Excel financial-analysis model
- Develop a management-level Power BI dashboard
- Prepare an executive financial-analysis report
- Present evidence-based observations and recommendations
- Publish the completed project through GitHub

## 11. Success Criteria

The project will be considered successful when:

- All extracted figures are traceable to the annual reports
- Reporting units and currencies are used consistently
- Financial-statement totals are validated
- Calculations are accurate and reproducible
- Year-over-year comparisons are clearly explained

- Charts communicate useful financial insights
- Conclusions are supported by evidence
- Limitations and assumptions are disclosed
- Repository files are clearly organized
- The project can be confidently explained during an interview

---

## 12. Initial Assumptions

- The published annual reports are reliable primary sources.
- Consolidated figures will be used rather than separate company-only figures.
- All monetary figures will be presented in euros unless stated otherwise.
- Figures will be entered in millions of euros for consistency.
- FY23, FY24 and FY25 represent financial years ending on 31 August.
- Comparative figures will be checked between annual reports before use.
- Ratios requiring average balances may use opening and closing balances when available.
- Ratios that cannot be calculated reliably will be excluded rather than estimated without support.

---

## 13. Known Limitations

- Inter IKEA Group is not identical to the entire global IKEA retail business.
- Many IKEA stores are operated by independent franchisees.
- Segment-level financial information may be limited.
- Some operational metrics may not be consistently available across all three years.
- Major ownership, financing or acquisition transactions may affect comparability.
- Financial results may be affected by foreign exchange, tariffs, inflation and supply-chain conditions.
- The analysis is based entirely on publicly available information.
