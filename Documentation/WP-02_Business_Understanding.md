## 1. Purpose of This Workpaper

The purpose of this workpaper is to document an understanding of Inter IKEA Group’s business model, operating structure, revenue sources, cost structure, working-capital drivers and major business risks.

This understanding will guide the financial-statement analysis and help connect changes in reported financial results with underlying business activities.

---

## 2. Entity Being Analysed

This project analyses:

**Inter IKEA Group**

The ultimate parent company is:

**Inter IKEA Holding B.V.**

Inter IKEA Holding B.V. is owned and controlled by Inter IKEA Foundation.

The analysis is based on the consolidated financial statements of Inter IKEA Group and does not represent the financial performance of every independently operated IKEA retail franchise.

## 4. Group Structure

Inter IKEA Group operates through three core business areas:

### 4.1 Retail Concept

Retail Concept is responsible for developing and protecting the IKEA Concept.

Its responsibilities include:

- Ownership and development of the IKEA Concept
- IKEA brand positioning
- Franchise-system development
- Retail-method development
- Marketing and communication
- Digital development
- Sustainability direction
- Support for IKEA franchisees

Inter IKEA Systems B.V. acts as the worldwide IKEA franchisor and owner of the IKEA Concept.

---

### 4.2 Range

The Range business is primarily responsible for developing and designing the IKEA product range.

Its activities include:

- Product design
- Product development
- Home-furnishing range development
- Food-product development
- Packaging solutions
- Range planning
- Product-quality improvement
- Affordability initiatives

IKEA of Sweden AB is a major entity within the Range business.

---

### 4.3 Supply

The Supply business manages sourcing, production, wholesale distribution and supply-chain operations.

Its activities include:

- Sourcing products from external suppliers
- Manufacturing selected IKEA products
- Managing purchasing activities
- Wholesale sales to IKEA franchisees
- Warehousing and distribution
- Transportation and logistics
- Supplier relationships
- Product components

The majority of IKEA products are sourced from external suppliers.

IKEA Industry manufactures a smaller portion of the total IKEA range, while IKEA Components supplies items such as screws, plugs and wooden dowels.

---

## 5. Franchise Model

Most IKEA retail locations are operated by independent franchisees.

Franchisees are responsible for:

- Running IKEA retail locations
- Selling IKEA products to customers
- Managing local business operations
- Marketing within their markets
- Developing local retail performance

Inter IKEA Group supports the franchise system through:

- Franchise rights
- Product-range development
- Wholesale product supply
- Brand and concept development
- Retail methods
- Strategic support

The Group earns income from franchise fees and the sale of products to franchisees.

---

## 6. Main Customers

Inter IKEA Group’s direct customers are primarily IKEA franchisees rather than individual retail consumers.

The franchisees purchase IKEA products through the Group’s wholesale supply activities and pay franchise fees for the right to operate under the IKEA Concept.

The final retail customers purchase products from IKEA franchisees.

The commercial flow can therefore be represented as:

Suppliers and Manufacturers  
↓  
Inter IKEA Group Supply Operations  
↓  
IKEA Franchisees  
↓  
Retail Customers

## 7. Revenue Model

Inter IKEA Group generates operating income mainly from:

### 7.1 Wholesale Sales

The Group purchases or manufactures IKEA products and sells them to IKEA franchisees.

Wholesale sales are influenced by:

- Product volumes
- Wholesale prices
- Retail demand
- Product availability
- Franchisee inventory requirements
- New-market expansion
- Supply-chain capacity

---

### 7.2 Franchise Fees

Franchisees pay fees for the right to operate under the IKEA Concept.

Franchise-fee income is linked to IKEA retail sales generated by franchisees.

Franchise fees may therefore be affected by:

- Retail sales value
- Product pricing
- Sales volumes
- Number of markets
- Number of retail locations
- Consumer demand

---

### 7.3 Other Operating Income

Other operating income may include income associated with:

- Intellectual-property activities
- Service arrangements
- Business-development activities
- Other Group operations

The final financial model will use the classifications disclosed in the consolidated profit and loss statement.

## 13. Major Strategic Developments

The following developments may affect comparability across FY23–FY25:

### FY23

- Ownership and control were transferred to Inter IKEA Foundation.
- A significant capital contribution affected equity and financing balances.
- Russian production operations were divested.
- Supply-chain costs and inventory levels remained important performance factors.

### FY24

- Wholesale prices were reduced to improve affordability.
- Raw-material and transportation costs declined.
- Treasury arrangements changed.
- Inventory levels reduced.
- Net profit improved compared with FY23.

### FY25

- IKEA Baltic retail and real-estate operations were acquired.
- Forestry assets in Latvia were acquired.
- Inventory increased to improve product availability.
- Tariffs and commodity-market uncertainty affected supply-chain costs.
- Lower interest income contributed to a decline in net profit.

These events should be considered when interpreting year-over-year financial movements. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7} :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8} :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

## 14. Key Business Risks

### 14.1 Supply-Chain Risk

The Group relies on a global network of suppliers, transport providers, warehouses and manufacturing facilities.

Supply-chain disruptions may affect:

- Product availability
- Inventory
- Transportation costs
- Revenue
- Customer demand
- Profit margins

---

### 14.2 Commodity-Price Risk

Changes in the price of wood, metals, energy and other inputs may affect product costs and profitability.

---

### 14.3 Foreign-Exchange Risk

The Group operates globally and is exposed to multiple currencies.

Currency movements may affect:

- Franchise fees
- Product purchases
- Product sales
- Financial income
- Financial expenses
- Reported profit

## 15. Initial Analyst Observations

Before conducting detailed calculations, the following observations have been identified:

1. Operating income may not move in the same direction as sales volume because wholesale-price reductions were introduced to improve affordability.

2. Profitability may be strongly influenced by raw-material, transportation and supply-chain costs.

3. FY23–FY25 comparisons must consider major financing, ownership and acquisition events.

4. Inventory is a significant working-capital driver and may materially affect operating cash flow.

5. A strong equity ratio does not automatically mean that operating performance improved.

6. Net profit may be influenced by financial income, interest rates, foreign-exchange movements and taxation in addition to operating performance.

7. Management commentary must be tested against reported figures rather than accepted without analysis.

---

## 16. Questions to Test During Financial Analysis

The Excel analysis should test the following:

- Did operating income decline after price reductions?
- Did lower product costs offset reduced operating income?
- Did operating margin improve or weaken?
- Why did net profit increase in FY24 and decline in FY25?
- Did inventory changes explain movements in operating cash flow?
- Did the Group’s equity position strengthen?
- Were acquisitions responsible for major asset changes?
- Was free cash flow sufficient to fund investments and dividends?
- Did asset efficiency improve?
- Which risks are most visible in the financial statements?

---

## 17. Conclusion

Inter IKEA Group operates a complex global franchise, product-development, manufacturing and wholesale-supply business.

Its financial performance is influenced by retail demand, pricing decisions, sales volumes, raw-material costs, transportation costs, inventory levels, acquisitions, foreign exchange, interest rates and international trade conditions.

This business understanding will be used to guide the financial-statement extraction, ratio selection, trend analysis and interpretation of results.
